PURE PU’ER TEA.

Reframing a tea tradition for a younger generation.

01

Project overview

A contemporary Pu’er tea brand and product project, conceived to bring a new generation closer to tea. The supplied presentation uses the name PURE PU’ER TEA and the headline “Find Your Mountain.”

The aim was to rethink what makes Pu’er compelling: inviting younger people to taste, appreciate and understand tea, while carrying forward its culture and history. The project also looked to standards in the coffee industry as a reference for connecting Pu’er with a wider international audience.

02

My role

I was responsible for product packaging, the brand logo and advertising design. My research included studying existing brands’ packaging and brand philosophies, alongside field visits. I used this research to identify ideas relevant to the brand’s own direction.

  • Product packaging design
  • Brand logo design
  • Advertising design
  • Brand and packaging research, including field visits
03

Design objectives

  • Make Pu’er relevant to a new generation.

    Rethink its selling points to spark younger audiences’ interest in tasting and learning about tea.

  • Carry tea culture forward.

    Connect a contemporary brand direction with the culture and history of Pu’er.

  • Look beyond the local market.

    Learn from standards in the coffee industry, with the ambition of making Pu’er more accessible to an international audience.

These are project goals, not claims of measured results.

04

Design approach

Research first. A direction informed by the category.

I explored how existing brands expressed their ideas through packaging and brand philosophy. Research and field visits helped me distil relevant references and consider how they could inform a distinct direction for the Pu’er brand.

Four origins. Four expressions.

The supplied image presents four origin-labelled variants: Bingdao, Yiwu, Jingmai and Lao Ban Zhang. Each has its own colour combination, while a shared layout brings the range together.

Mountains, leaves and a shared identity.

Mountain silhouettes and botanical forms carry across the outer boxes and individual sachets. Bold typography, bilingual origin names and numbered labels create a consistent visual system. The labels also show sweetness and bitterness scales alongside tasting descriptors.

These visible elements connect with the headline “Find Your Mountain” and the line “A Taste of the East,” framing the collection around its different origins.

This description reflects what is visible in the supplied image. The reasons behind the visual choices, the basis for the flavour ratings, and any connection to specific coffee-industry standards still need confirmation.
